<p>The VIX® Index is a financial benchmark designed to be an up-to-the minute market estimate of expected volatility in the S&amp;P 500 Index and is calculated by using the midpoint of real-time S&amp;P 500® Index (SPX) option bid/ask quotes.&nbsp;The VIX Index generally tends to have an inverse relationship with the S&amp;P 500 Index and historically when the S&amp;P 500 Index declines the VIX Index generally tends to increase. Simply put, the VIX Index tells us the level of expected volatility of the S&amp;P 500 Index for the next 30 days, with a 68% confidence level, or a one standard deviation of the normal probability curve.</p>th<p>Although the VIX Index isn’t expressed as a percentage, it can be understood as one. For example, if the VIX Index is at 28, this represents an expected <em>annualized</em> change with a 68% probability of less than 28% in the S&amp;P 500 Index, <strong>in either direction.&nbsp;</strong>With the S&amp;P 500 Index trading at 3,900 (as of June 10), this correlated into a projected range of 2,808 – 4,992 over the next year.&nbsp;Calculating potential movement in the S&amp;P 500 Index using the VIX Index on a shorter basis is a bit more complicated.&nbsp;The expected volatility for a single month can be calculated from this figure by dividing the VIX Index level of 28 not by 12 (since there are 12 months in a year), but by the square root of 12 (approximately 3.46).&nbsp;This would imply a move of 8.09% over the next month or a range of approximately 3,585 – 4,215 during that time frame.&nbsp;Similarly, expected volatility for a week would be 28 divided by the square root of 52 or 7.21.&nbsp;This implies a move of 3.88% over the next week or a range of approximately 3,749 – 4,051.&nbsp;(Source: <a href="https://sixfigureinvesting.com/2014/06/volatility-and-the-square-root-of-time/">Six Figure Investing</a>)</p><p>Since the VIX Index uses calendar day annualization (365 days) rather than many historical/realized volatility measurements which use trading day annualization (252 days), the daily expected volatility is calculated by taking the current VIX Index level divided by the square root of 365.&nbsp;With the VIX Index at 28, the daily expected volatility is 28 divided by 19.1.&nbsp;This implies a daily move of approximately 1.46%.&nbsp;As mentioned above, while the VIX Index generally tends to move in the opposite direction of the S&amp;P 500 Index, this relationship is not always maintained.&nbsp;In fact, about 20% of the time, the VIX Index and S&amp;P 500 Index move in the same direction. (Source: <a href="https://www.macroption.com/vix-spx-correlation/#summary">macroption</a>)</p><p>Using data from <a href="https://www.barchart.com/">Barchart</a> I created a comparison of the daily move in the S&amp;P 500 Index (close to close) to the VIX Index, from January 3 – June 10 of this year.&nbsp;The average daily move in absolute terms in the S&amp;P 500 Index during this time was approximately 1.24% which correlates to a VIX Index level of slightly lower than 24. (24/19.1).&nbsp;The average daily reading for the VIX Index during the same period was 26.04.</p><h2>S&amp;P 500 Index vs. VIX Index Implied Move</h2>Source: Barchart<p>In summary, the comparison
