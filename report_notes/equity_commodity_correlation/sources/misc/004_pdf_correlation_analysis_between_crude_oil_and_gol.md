Abstract. The current market has a close link between CL/GC, with a possible 
correlation. The fluctuations in crude oil prices may somewhat affect the gold 
prices. This study utilises SPSSPRO to perform DLS regression analysis on crude 
oil, gold, and other variables to derive correlation results. The research collected 
data of crude oil, gold and related macroeconomic factors in the past 10 years for 
analysis and research and obtained the average value and other relevant data. Finally, the path chart of relevant variables was obtained to see the correlation between dependent variables, independent variables and control variables more 
clearly. The study finds a positive correlation between crude oil and gold prices, 
meaning that when crude oil prices rise, gold prices also rise, and when crude oil 
prices fall, gold prices fall. Other macroeconomic factors also correlate with gold 
prices, such as rate of inflation, US federal interest rate and exchange rate (US), 
these macroeconomic factors all have a high or low impact on gold, but the degree of correlation varies.
Keywords: Crude oil; Gold; Macro environment; Regression analyze
1 Introduction
In recent years, gold has gained increasing prominence in global markets. Its purchase 
has become a trend, and its price has attracted greater attention from investors and the 
general public. Gold is widely used as a precious metal in various fields due to its exceptional ductility and corrosion resistance. Moreover, gold is a significant asset that 
can be readily converted into currency at any time and place, making it highly valued 
by individuals and institutions. Given its crucial characteristics, gold is vital as a store 
of value and a hedging asset in the international financial market. Today, gold continues 
to hold a pivotal position worldwide, attracting sustained investment interest.
Gold is considered a safe-haven asset, meaning it helps mitigate significant losses 
incurred by investors during periods of market volatility. When stock markets decline 
or currencies depreciate, investors often turn to gold as a hedge asset, preserving assets 
during crises. This research explores the intricate link between gold resource and crude 
oil prices, shedding light on how these critical commodities interact in the global market.

Crude oil, a non-renewable fossil fuel, is the most fundamental material for modern
industry and is used extensively in various manufacturing sectors. Numerous factors
influence its price, including technological advancements, supply and demand dynamics, and geopolitical events. The significant fluctuations in crude oil prices and its widespread application in industrial construction profoundly impact the global economy. To
some extent, these fluctuations also affect gold prices, creating a complex interplay
between the two commodities.
The price of gold is influenced by multiple factors, including the US dollar exchange
rate, stock market performance, inflation rates, interest rates, and oil prices [1]. This
research highlights the interconnection between crude oil and gold resource prices, suggesting more precise relationship exists between them. Specifically, this study provides
valuable insights into market trends and operating mechanisms, offering policymakers
and investors a clearer understanding of making informed decisions to reduce investment risks.
Previous studies have explored the relationships between the US dollar and various
factors such as exchange rates, stock market performance, inflation rates, interest rates,
and oil prices. Building on this foundation, the current study delves deeper into the comovement of gold and crude oil prices. This research considers crude oil prices as the
independent variable and Gold prices that serve as the dependent variable, utilising Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression to measure the strength and nature of their relationship.
2 Literature Review
There may be a certain relationship between gold and oil. After the collapse of the
Bretton Woods system in the 1970s, the price of gold and crude oil experienced significant increases. In the 21st century, with the continuous and steady growth of the economy, both gold and the crude oil have risen synchronously. This research reveals a
statistically meaningful linkage between gold and crude oil markets, as evidenced by
the two key indicators.
Firstly, there is a certain correlation between crude oil and the global economic level.
The financial market can predict the price of crude oil. The price of crude oil is measured in US dollars, and the US dollar index can reflect the fluctuations of US dollar
price in the international forex arena. Since the price of crude oil is measured in US
dollars, the trend of US dollar fluctuations also affects the price of crude oil [2]. Therefore, the financial market can affect the price of crude oil to a certain extent. Specifically, there is an inverse relationship between the US dollar index and the price of crude
oil [3]. When the price of the US dollar rises, the price of crude oil falls; when the price
of the US dollar falls, the price of crude oil rises [2]. Therefore, rather than two unrelated individuals, a certain correlation exists between the financial market and crude oil
prices. When the US dollar appreciates, crude oil becomes more expensive, decreasing
market demand for oil and its price. Conversely, when the US dollar devalues, the demand for oil increases, leading to an increase in its price. The depreciation of the US

dollar also means that investors tend to shift their assets to anti-inflation assets such as
gold and commodities, which indicates inflation.
Secondly, there is also a certain relationship between inflation and gold. There is a
positive correlation between the supply of US dollars and the price of gold in the long
run. The supply of US dollars affects the price of gold mainly through inflation and
exchange rates [4]. When inflation occurs, the demand for gold increases as a good
hedging asset and value-preserving product to mitigate the inflation crisis [4]. Therefore, when investors expect inflation to occur, they buy gold and other hedging products. When the US dollar devalues, when the exchange rate decreases, gold becomes
more popular with investors, increasing demand for gold and thus raising its price. Empirical analysis shows that the long-term weakness of the US dollar is related to the
simultaneous appreciation of these two commodities as investors adopt a dual hedging
strategy: buying gold to preserve value.
In summary, there may be a certain correlation between oil and gold. From previous
research, it can be seen that the US dollar price may be influenced by a series of related

index). In this study, the price dynamics between crude oil and gold commodities was
mainly analysed and studied to determine whether the two commodities exhibit cointegrated price behavior during different periods. Relevant conclusions were drawn by
analysing the relationship between the two using models and software. So, we can speculate on the relevant conclusion that there is a positive correlation between oil and gold.

The data studied in this research, such as inflation, the United States federal funds rate
and exchange rate (dollar index), are important indicators of the macro-financial markets; changes in inflation in the market for economic changes in an indicator that the
economy is in a growth stage, will lead to price increases to affect the price of gold,

6 Conclusion
The following points are concluded based on the research on the relationship between
crude oil and gold.
According to regression model analysis, CL/GC have a positive correlation, with
crude oil prices influencing gold prices. Oil inflation can indicate gold inflation. Previous studies have also shown a tendency for both to fluctuate in unison. This passage
verifies the authenticity of the results.
Secondly, regression analysis reveals that the inflation rate, exchange rate (US dollar
index), and the U.S. federal funds rate all have certain effects on gold. Inflation hurts
gold, indicating that gold prices may fall when the market's inflation rate rises, serving
as a tool for hedging.
Thirdly, the U.S. federal funds rate positively affects gold, reflecting complex macroeconomic environments, including high inflation expectations and increased demand
for hedging. This phenomenon suggests that multiple factors influence gold prices and
cannot be predicted solely based on one variable. A comprehensive analysis with more
data is necessary.
Lastly, there is a positive correlation between the exchange rate and gold prices. An
increase in the exchange rate is accompanied by an increase in gold prices, possibly
indicating that increased hedging demand leads to higher gold prices.
