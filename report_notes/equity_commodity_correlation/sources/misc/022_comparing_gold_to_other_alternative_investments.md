<div> <div> <div> <p><b>Bloomberg Commodity Index<br> </b>A broadly diversified commodity price index distributed by Bloomberg Indexes that tracks 22 commodity futures and seven sectors. No one commodity can compose less than 2 percent or more than 15 percent of the index, and no sector can represent more than 33 percent of the index.</p> <p><b>Bitcoin</b><br> A peer-to-peer digital currency created in 2009 that offers the promise of lower transaction fees than those of traditional online payment mechanisms. Unlike government-issued currencies, Bitcoin is run and “regulated” by its own users using an infrastructure called “blockchain.”</p> <p><b>Commodities</b><br> A basic good used in commerce that is interchangeable, or “fungible,” with other commodities of the same type. Commodities are most often used as inputs in the production of other goods or services For example, crude oil is a commodity that is used to make motor fuels, heating oil and lubricants.</p> <p><b>Correlation</b><br> The historical tendency of two investments to move together. Investors often combine investments with low correlations to diversify portfolios.</p> <p><b>Credit Suisse Hedge Fund Index<br> </b>The Credit Suisse Hedge Fund Index was the industry's first, and remains the leading, asset-weighted hedge fund index. The Index uses a rules-based construction methodology, identifies its constituent funds, and minimizes subjectivity in the index member selection process as a result of the rules-based method. It aims to achieve maximum representation of the index universe.</p> <p><b>Diversification</b><br> A strategy of combining a broad mix of investments and asset classes to potentially limit risk, although diversification does not guarantee protection against a loss in falling markets.</p> <p><b>Diversification Benefits<br> </b>In modern portfolio theory, diversification is an approach used to potentially reduce the overall risk of the portfolio by holding a mix of assets with low correlations to each other. The potential benefit of holding uncorrelated assets is that some investments may rise while others fall.</p> <p><b>Downside Risk<br> </b>A given security’s potential to lose value if a prevailing market trend suddenly changes. The term also refers to the specific financial amount of the “worst-case” loss that that can occur in such sudden shifts.</p> <p><b>Drawdown</b><br> A specific decline in the stock market during a specific time period that is measured in percentage terms as a peak-to-trough move.</p> <p><b>Economic Cycle<br> </b>Periods of growth or contraction in the economy, typically called periods of expansion or recession. Different sectors and industries typically perform differently based on particular phases of the economy cycle.</p> <p><b>Eureka&nbsp;Hedge Long Short Equities Hedge Fund Index<br> </b>Tracks the&nbsp;performance of long-short equity hedge funds. A long-short (L/S) equity&nbsp;strategy involves both buying stocks expected to rise in value (long position)&nbsp;and shorting stocks expected to fall (short position).</p> <p><b>FTSE NAREIT All Equity REITS Index<br> </b>The index is a free-float-adjusted market capitalization-weighted index that includes all tax-qualified REITs listed in the NYSE, AMEX, and NASDAQ National Market.</p> <p><b>Inflation</b><br> An overall increase in the price of an economy’s goods and services during a given period, translating to a loss in purchasing power per unit of currency. Inflation generally occurs when growth of the money supply outpaces growth of the economy. Central banks attempt to limit inflation — and avoid deflation — in order to keep the economy running smoothly.</p> <p><b>Liquid Alternative Trading Strategy</b></p> <p>Alternative investment approaches, including real estate, commodities, private equity, distressed securities and hedge funds that are available through relatively liquid structures such as ETFs, mutual funds and closed-end funds.</p> <p><b>LBMA Gold Price <br> </b>The LBMA Gold Price is determined twice each business day — 10:30 a.m. London time (i.e., the LBMA Gold Price AM) and 3:00 p.m. London time (i.e., the LBMA Gold Price PM) by the participants in a physically settled, electronic and tradable auction.</p> <p><b>Lognormal Returns<br> </b>An investment return that, when its natural logarithm is taken, results&nbsp;in value follows a normal distribution.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p> <p><b>Morningstar&nbsp;PitchBook All Global<br> </b>A Morningstar&nbsp;Pitchbook index that tracks the performance of all global listed private equity&nbsp;companies</p> <p><b>Risk-Adjusted Return<br> </b>A risk-based profitability measurement framework for analyzing risk-adjusted financial performance; it is designed to provide a consistent view of profitability across different assets.</p> <p><b>Rogers International Commodity Index<br> </b>The index represents the value of a basket of 36 commodity futures contracts. The index is a composite, US dollar-based total&nbsp;return index launched by James B. Rogers on July 31, 1998. The index represents&nbsp;the value of a basket of futures contracts on commodities consumed in the&nbsp;global economy, ranging from agricultural to energy and metals products.</p> <p><b>Spot Gold Price<br> </b>The price in spot markets for gold. In US dollar terms, spot gold is referred to with the symbol “XAU,” which refers to the price of one troy ounce of gold in USD terms.</p> <p><b>S&amp;P GSCI Total Return Index<br> </b>The S&amp;P GSCI Total Return Index in USD is widely recognized as the leading measure of general commodity price movements and inflation in the world economy. The index is calculated primarily on a world production-weighted basis comprised of the principal physical commodities futures contracts.</p> <p><b>S&amp;P 500® Total Return Index<br> </b>The version of the popular benchmark for U.S. large-cap equities that includes 500 companies from leading industries and captures about 80% coverage of available market capitalization in the US that reflects returns after reinvestment of dividends.</p> <p><b>Tail Risk<br> </b>A type of portfolio risk associated with unforeseen events that lead to sharp declines in equities and a rush to safe-haven investments such as short-dated Treasuries or gold.</p> <p><b>Up Market Capture<br> </b>The percentage return that an asset captures when a market benchmark is up.</p> <p><b>Volatility<br> </b>The tendency of a market index or security to jump around in price. Volatility is typically expressed as the annualized standard deviation of returns. In modern portfolio theory, securities with higher volatility are generally seen as riskier due to higher potential losses.<br> </p> </div> </div> </div> <!-- END Provide a slot to drop in Accordion Components -->
