# Is There Seasonality in Forex?

Traders often wonder whether there is a seasonality to the currency markets and if there is, how it looks. The summer season is usually associated with the whipsawing sideways market that offers few opportunities for day trading. In stock trading, they say: "Sell in May and go away." Forex traders rarely employ buy-and-hold strategies, though seasonal factors should influence currencies too.

## Seasonal Trends in Gains and Losses

There is no lack of Forex seasonality studies showing the tendencies of currency pairs to gain or lose during particular months based on statistics of past years. However, the implications of those studies are of little practical value.

**Example:** Kathy Lien finds some USD/JPY seasonal cyclicity:
- October was bullish in 68% of samples during 1999-2018
- August was least bullish, showing gains only in 32% of samples

However, two subsequent years proved such analysis unreliable:
- USD/JPY was almost unchanged in October 2019 and August 2020
- It fell in October 2020, contrary to the seasonal pattern

## Seasonality of Volatility

**This is where seasonality becomes important.** Daily ranges (average and median) seem to be narrowing during some months and widening during others. There is a fundamental difference in how major currency pairs behave during spring, summer, autumn, and winter.

### Key Concepts

**Average Daily Range (ADR):** Mean number of pips per day the pair has been moving during the period. High ADR implies more market movement and more intraday volatility to profit from.

**Average Hourly Range (AHR):** Provides additional information about the nature of currency rates volatility. When ADR is significantly higher than AHR, it means rapid single moves; lower difference implies more homogeneous distribution.

**Median Daily Range (MDR):** What range traders are more likely to encounter. ADR gets skewed easily with outlying values.

**Median Hourly Range (MHR):** Complements daily range statistics.

## Seasonality Cycles by Currency

### EUR, GBP, JPY, CHF

**Most Active Months:** January and March

**Lowest-Range Months:**
- EUR/USD: July and October (median), April, July, October (average)
- GBP/USD: May and August (average), July and August (median)
- USD/JPY: November and December
- USD/CHF: April and July

**Overall Pattern:** Most active in winter and spring, less active in summer and autumn

### CAD, AUD, NZD, SEK

**USD/CAD:**
- Hourly range maximized in January
- Daily ranges good in June and October
- Bottoms in July and December

**AUD/USD and NZD/USD:**
- Trade wider in September and October
- Lowest in December, partially April and July
- **Inverse seasonality to EUR/GBP/JPY/CHF** - summer shows most activity, winter the least (expected for Southern Hemisphere currencies)

**USD/SEK:**
- Peaks in March
- Worst in January, February, October
- Prefers spring and summer

## Key Insights

1. **Median values are always lower than average** - More outlying wide ranges than narrow ranges exist
2. **Percentage ranges enable meaningful comparison** between currency pairs
3. **Southern Hemisphere currencies** show inverse seasonality to Northern Hemisphere

## Practical Application

- ADR analysis helps understand expected daily movement
- AHR vs ADR comparison reveals if moves are gradual or sudden
- MDR gives more realistic expectations than ADR
- Combine volatility seasonality with trend analysis
