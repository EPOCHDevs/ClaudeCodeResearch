Title: A Long-Term View Of U.S. Treasury Yields
Topic: 2-Year and 10-Year U.S. Treasury yields over the long term since 1988. This chart tracks the historical path of key benchmark interest rates that reflect borrowing costs and investor expectations about economic growth, inflation, and Federal Reserve policy.
Question: How have the 2-year and 10-year U.S. Treasury yields behaved over the past several decades, and where do they stand now?
Key Takeaways:
- As of February 27, 2026, the 10-year Treasury yield was 3.98% and the 2-year Treasury yield was 3.39%, both well below the peaks seen in the early 1990s but above the historic lows of the 2020 era.
- The long-term trend shows a secular decline in yields from the late 1980s through 2020, followed by a sharp reversal upward during 2022-2023 as the Federal Reserve aggressively raised rates to combat inflation.
- The levels of the 10-year and 2-year yields provide a window into market sentiment about future economic conditions -- higher yields generally indicate growing confidence in economic stability and inflation expectations, while lower yields can signal concerns about weaker growth.
