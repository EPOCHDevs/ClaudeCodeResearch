Title: What If You Only Invested at Market Tops?
Topic: This chart shows the annualized return of the S&P 500 from the peak of every bull market since 1950 through today, demonstrating that even investing at the worst possible timing has still yielded positive long-term returns.
Question: What would your annualized return be if you invested in the S&P 500 only at the peak of every bull market since 1950?
Key Takeaways:
- Even with perfectly bad timing, long-term returns have been strong: investing only at market tops since 1950 still resulted in positive annualized returns in every single case, ranging from approximately 6.2% to 12.5%.
- The average annualized return across all 11 bull market peaks is approximately 8.3%, reinforcing the power of long-term compounding regardless of entry point.
- The data makes a compelling case for staying invested, emphasizing that even less-than-ideal entry points have generated positive annualized returns historically.
