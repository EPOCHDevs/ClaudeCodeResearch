Title: The Impact of Bad Investing Behavior
Topic: This chart compares the growth of $1,000 in the S&P 500 since 1990 under two scenarios: staying invested through all fluctuations versus selling every 5% pullback and buying back at the next all-time high, revealing the devastating cost of reactive investing behavior.
Question: What is the long-term impact of selling during every 5% pullback and buying back at the next all-time high, compared to staying invested?
Key Takeaways:
- A $1,000 investment that remained untouched and stayed invested through all fluctuations since 1990 would have grown to $19,465, while the same investment using a sell-at-5%-pullback-and-buy-at-all-time-high strategy would have only grown to approximately $3,510.
- The reactive strategy of selling during pullbacks and waiting for recovery causes investors to miss out on the market's recovery phases, resulting in dramatically slower growth over time.
- The chart powerfully illustrates that remaining invested through market fluctuations has historically led to far stronger long-term outcomes than strategies based on frequent buying and selling, reinforcing the importance of a disciplined, long-term approach.
