Title: S&P 500 Performance After Two 20%+ Up Years
Topic: This chart examines the S&P 500's annual return in the year following back-to-back years of 20%+ gains since 1926, showing whether strong momentum tends to continue or reverse.
Question: What typically happens to the S&P 500 the year after it posts two consecutive years of 20%+ returns?
Key Takeaways:
- Since 1926, there have been several instances of back-to-back 20%+ years (1937, 1956, 1997, 1998, 1999, 2025), and the following year's performance has varied widely, from a devastating -38.59% (1937) to a strong +31.01% (1997).
- The average return in the year following two consecutive 20%+ up years has been approximately +18.39%, suggesting the market has more often continued to deliver positive returns than not after such streaks.
- Even following periods of strong growth, the potential for continued positive returns remains, underscoring the value of staying invested rather than trying to time a correction.
