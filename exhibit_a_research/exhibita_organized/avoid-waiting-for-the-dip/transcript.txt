Title: Avoid Waiting for "the Dip"
Topic: This chart tracks the number of trading days between each 5% pullback in the S&P 500 since 1950, showing that the intervals are highly unpredictable and can stretch for extended periods, making it impractical to wait for a dip before investing.
Question: How long do investors typically have to wait between 5% pullbacks in the S&P 500, and is waiting for a dip a reliable strategy?
Key Takeaways:
- The time between 5% pullbacks in the S&P 500 is highly variable and unpredictable, with gaps ranging from just a few days to over 400 trading days, making it extremely difficult to time the market effectively.
- As of the chart's latest data point, it had been 71 trading days since the last 5% pullback, but there is no reliable pattern to predict when the next one will occur.
- A strategy based on waiting for market corrections can lead to missed opportunities; investors who adopt a long-term, buy-and-hold approach are often less concerned with short-term market movements and focus instead on broader economic trends and company fundamentals.
