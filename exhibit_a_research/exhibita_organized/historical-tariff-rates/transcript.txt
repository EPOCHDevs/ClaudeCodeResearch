Title: U.S. Average Effective Tariff Rate
Topic: This chart displays the U.S. average effective tariff rate historically and implied from 1790 through 2025, showing how tariffs have evolved from a major revenue source to a diminished trade policy tool and their recent resurgence.
Question: How have U.S. tariff rates changed over the past 200+ years, and where do current implied rates stand in historical context?
Key Takeaways:
- Tariffs have been part of U.S. trade policy for over 200 years: In the 1800s, they were a major source of government revenue, often exceeding 30%, but as the economy evolved and global trade expanded, average tariff rates steadily declined.
- The Budget Lab at Yale estimates an implied average effective tariff rate of 16.8% based on policy proposals as of November 17th, 2025, which is a hypothetical calculation representing the potential average across affected imports.
- Historical context helps investors stay focused on long-term goals rather than short-term noise: Trade policies like tariffs can impact the cost of goods, company profits, and the overall economy, but looking at the bigger picture helps keep things in perspective.
