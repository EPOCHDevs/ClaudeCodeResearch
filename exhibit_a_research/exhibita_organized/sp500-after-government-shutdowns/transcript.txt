Title: How Has The S&P 500 Performed During Government Shutdowns?
Topic: This chart tracks how the S&P 500 performed during all past U.S. government shutdowns since 1980, showing the market return during each shutdown period.
Question: Do government shutdowns historically lead to negative stock market returns?
Key Takeaways:
- In 8 of the past 11 shutdowns since 1980, the S&P 500 actually generated a positive return, showing that markets have often looked past the political noise.
- The largest positive return during a shutdown was approximately 10.27% (Jan 2018), while the worst was about -2.09% (Oct 1990), indicating returns have varied widely but skewed positive.
- Short-term political events like government shutdowns have not consistently dictated market direction, reinforcing that tuning out the noise has historically been more effective than reacting to it.
