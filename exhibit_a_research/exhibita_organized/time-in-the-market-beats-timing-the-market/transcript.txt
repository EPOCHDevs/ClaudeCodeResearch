Title: "Time In" Has Historically Beat "Timing" the Market
Topic: This chart compares the growth of $1 invested in the S&P 500 since 1950 across three scenarios: staying fully invested, missing the best 50 days, and missing the best 100 days, illustrating the enormous cost of missing the market's best-performing days.
Question: How much does missing the best days in the market impact long-term investment growth?
Key Takeaways:
- A $1 investment in the S&P 500 since 1950, staying fully invested through February 2026, would have grown to $413; missing the best 50 days reduces that to only $29, and missing the best 100 days leaves just $3.5.
- The dramatic difference between staying invested ($413) versus missing key days ($29 or $3.5) demonstrates that a buy-and-hold strategy vastly outperforms attempts to time the market.
- Investors who focus on maintaining consistent exposure to the market, rather than trying to time entry and exit points, have historically seen significantly more growth.
