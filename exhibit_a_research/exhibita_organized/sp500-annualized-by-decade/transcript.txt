Title: Recall, The 2000s Were a Lost Decade
Topic: This chart shows the annualized S&P 500 return by decade since 1950, illustrating the wide variation in long-term equity performance across different decades including the negative returns of the 2000s.
Question: How have S&P 500 annualized returns varied by decade, and how does the 2000s compare to other periods?
Key Takeaways:
- The 2000s delivered a negative annualized return of approximately -2.6% for the S&P 500, shaped by two major crises (the dotcom bubble and the Great Financial Crisis), making it a truly "lost decade" for equity investors.
- Most other decades were positive, with the 1950s returning about 13.7%, the 1990s at roughly 15.1%, and the 2010s at approximately 11.1%, showing that positive decades are the norm but not guaranteed.
- Decade-level returns show how outcomes can vary widely over time, reinforcing that positive returns are never a guarantee and that long-term investing requires patience through periods of weak or negative performance.
