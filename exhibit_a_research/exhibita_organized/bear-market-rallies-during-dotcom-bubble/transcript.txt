Title: Beware of Bear Market Rallies: 10 in Dotcom Bubble
Topic: This chart tracks the S&P 500 price during the 2000-2002 dotcom bubble decline and highlights the 10 distinct bear market rallies of at least 5% that occurred within the broader downtrend, illustrating how sharp rebounds can mislead investors during extended declines.
Question: How frequently did significant bear market rallies occur during the dotcom bubble, and what does that teach us about interpreting sharp rebounds?
Key Takeaways:
- Bear market rallies were frequent during the dotcom bubble: During the 2000-2002 decline, the S&P 500 experienced 10 distinct rallies of 5% or more, even as the broader market trend remained downward, with the index falling from roughly 1,500 to around 800.
- The S&P 500 recorded 10 distinct rallies of 5% or greater before ultimately continuing its broader decline, highlighting how repeated sharp rebounds can occur within extended downturns and do not necessarily signal a lasting market recovery.
- History shows that sharp rallies are a normal part of extended market downturns: Helping clients understand this dynamic can set better expectations, encourage a more disciplined long-term approach, and reduce the temptation to chase short-term moves during periods of volatility.
