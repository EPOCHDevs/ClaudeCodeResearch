Title: Historically, All-Time Highs Tend to Cluster
Topic: This chart shows the number of new all-time highs in the S&P 500 annually since 1990. It illustrates that all-time highs are not rare anomalies but tend to occur in clusters during strong market periods.
Question: How frequently does the S&P 500 reach new all-time highs, and do they tend to cluster together?
Key Takeaways:
- All-time highs in the S&P 500 have historically clustered together, with years like 1995, 2014, 2017, and 2021 seeing especially high concentrations of new record closes.
- In 2026, the S&P 500 has already hit all-time highs on 4 days year-to-date, continuing the pattern of clustering.
- While all-time highs may seem concerning to investors, the clustering pattern is a reminder that the S&P 500 has trended upward over time and new highs are a normal feature of long-term market growth.
