Title: Valuations Are a Solid Predictor in the Long-Term
Topic: This scatter plot charts the S&P 500's forward P/E ratio against its subsequent 10-year forward annualized return since 1990, demonstrating the inverse relationship between starting valuations and long-term equity returns.
Question: Does the S&P 500's forward P/E ratio reliably predict long-term returns, and what does the current valuation imply for future performance?
Key Takeaways:
- There is a clear inverse relationship: higher forward P/E ratios at the time of investment have historically been associated with lower 10-year forward annualized returns, and vice versa.
- Periods where the forward P/E was below 12x generally produced 10-year annualized returns of 10-18%, while forward P/Es above 20x have historically corresponded to returns closer to 0-6%.
- With the current S&P 500 forward P/E near 21.5x, the historical data suggests more muted long-term annualized returns over the next decade, reinforcing the importance of valuation awareness for long-term investors.
