Title: Most Years are Not "Average" for the S&P 500
Topic: This chart sorts all S&P 500 annual returns from highest to lowest since 1928, highlighting the years where returns fell between 5% and 10% to show how rare "average" return years actually are.
Question: How often does the S&P 500 actually deliver a return close to its long-term average of roughly 5-10%?
Key Takeaways:
- Most years' returns are either much higher or much lower than the "average" return of 5-10%, with the distribution far from evenly spread out.
- There have only been about six instances where the S&P 500 returned between 5% and 10%, demonstrating that "average" return years are extremely rare.
- The chart makes clear that investors should expect wide variability in any given year and not anchor expectations to the historical average annual return.
