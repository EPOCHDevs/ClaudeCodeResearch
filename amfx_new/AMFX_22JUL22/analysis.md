# AMFX 22JUL22 - Soft leads hard (usually)

**Date:** Friday, July 22, 2022
**Author:** Brent Donnelly, Spectra Markets
**Pages:** 5

## Summary

This newsletter examines the divergence between soft economic data (surveys, sentiment) and hard economic data (employment, output), arguing that soft data usually leads hard data. Donnelly ranks ISM New Orders and Initial Claims by percentile since 1996 and charts them together. The issue also covers SEC charges against Coinbase insiders for crypto insider trading, BOJ yield curve control (YCC) expectations, and the decline of time spent with friends. The main thesis: global soft data is uniformly and rapidly weakening, which is bearish for US employment and inflation.

**Current Views mentioned:** 1-month 0.99 EURUSD digital put at 18% (expires August 18).

## Kept Items - Detailed Analysis

### Item 1: Initial Claims vs ISM New Orders Percentile Ranking (1996-2022)

- **type:** Dual time series line chart
- **image_file:** page_01_img_03.png
- **title:** Initial Claims vs ISM New Orders (percentile ranking, 1996-2022)
- **page:** 1
- **data:** Monthly percentile rankings of Initial Claims and ISM New Orders since 1996. Each month is ranked according to its percentile within the full historical series.
- **assets/instruments:** N/A (macro indicators, not specific securities)
- **transforms/calculations:** Percentile ranking of each monthly reading within the full time series (1996-2022). Higher values = strong employment / strong new orders. Lower values = weak employment / weak new orders.
- **metrics:** Annotated with letter labels (A through I) marking key turning points:
  - A: 1999/2000 - ISM new orders peaked Nov 1999, great lead indicator
  - B: 2005 ISM false alarm (mid-cycle)
  - C: 2006 ISM early warning
  - D: 2007/2008 sentiment good lead indicator
  - E: 2009/2010 excellent bullish lead indicator at the turn
  - F: 2013 ISM false alarm (taper tantrum, slowdown fear)
  - G: 2015/2016 ISM false alarm (China deval, global growth dump)
  - H: 2019 ISM good lead indicator (but COVID wildcard)
  - I: Current (mid-2022) - TBD but not looking bueno
- **time_range:** 1996 to July 2022
- **x_axis:** Year (1996, 1998, 2000, ..., 2022)
- **y_axis:** Percentile ranking (implied 0-100 scale, not labeled)
- **insight:** Soft data (ISM New Orders) almost always leads hard data (Initial Claims) at economic turning points. The current reading (I) shows both indicators deteriorating, suggesting the base case is that US employment and inflation are about to turn aggressively weaker. Historical false alarms (B, F, G) occurred mid-cycle; true signals (A, C-D, E, H) preceded major economic shifts.
- **source:** ISM (New Orders), US Department of Labor (Initial Claims), charted by Spectra Markets

### Item 2: Japan YCC Survey Results

- **type:** Horizontal bar chart
- **image_file:** page_03_img_00.png
- **title:** "Japan's YCC for the rest of 2022 will:"
- **page:** 3
- **data:** Poll results from Fred Goodwin's weekly survey on BOJ yield curve control expectations
- **assets/instruments:** Japanese Government Bonds (JGBs), BOJ policy
- **transforms/calculations:** Simple percentage breakdown of survey responses
- **metrics:**
  - Remain unchanged: 51.5%
  - Get Tweaked: 30.3%
  - Be Abandoned: 18.2%
- **time_range:** Survey conducted week of July 18-22, 2022
- **x_axis:** Percentage (0% to 55%)
- **y_axis:** Three categories: Remain unchanged, Get Tweaked, Be Abandoned
- **insight:** Market participants have mostly given up on the end of YCC in 2022. The majority (51.5%) expect no change, with only 18.2% expecting full abandonment. - **source:** Fred Goodwin weekly poll
