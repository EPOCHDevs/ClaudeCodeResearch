# AMFX 30MAR23 - Dedollarization is not a thing

**Date:** March 30, 2023
**Author:** Brent Donnelly, Spectra Markets
**Pages:** 6 (including disclaimer)

## Summary

The newsletter dismantles the recurring "dedollarization" narrative, arguing that while there are periodic media hysteria cycles about the death of the USD, the data shows the dollar's share of international transactions is remarkably stable. The author presents BIS data showing USD dominance in FX transactions is steady, central bank reserve data showing USD share is stable, and a long-term Dollar Index chart showing no structural decline. The key distinction made: fiat currency erosion vs. hard assets (BTC, gold, real estate) is real and happening, but dedollarization (replacement of USD by another currency) is not. The piece also includes a brief section on AUD weakness relative to its traditional drivers (copper, gold, Hang Seng).

### Chart 1: USD, EUR, CNY, GBP and JPY -- Share of International Transactions (1989 to now)

- **type:** Grouped bar chart (5 currencies, 12 time periods)
- **image_file:** page_02_img_00.png
- **title:** USD, EUR, CNY, GBP and JPY: Share of international transactions (1989 to now)
- **page:** 2
- **data:** Currency share of global FX transactions from BIS Triennial Survey data
- **assets/instruments:** USD, EUR, CNY, GBP, JPY
- **transforms/calculations:** Percentage share of total FX transactions (note: every currency transaction has two sides, so grand total = 200%)
- **metrics:**
  - USD: consistently 80-90% share across all periods, remarkably stable
  - EUR: peaked ~35-40% in early 2000s, declining to ~30% by 2022
  - JPY: declining from ~25% to ~15-17%
  - GBP: stable around 10-15%
  - CNY: growing from near-zero to ~7% by 2022
- **time_range:** 1989 to 2022 (BIS triennial survey years)
- **x_axis:** Survey years (1989, 1992, 1995, 1998, 2001, 2004, 2007, 2010, 2013, 2016, 2019, 2022)
- **y_axis:** Percentage share (0-100%)
- **insight:** The USD's share of international FX transactions is remarkably stable at ~88% and shows no structural decline. If anything, EUR and JPY are losing share, not USD. CNY is gaining at the expense of EUR/JPY, not at USD's expense. This directly refutes the dedollarization narrative.
- **source:** BIS (Bank for International Settlements) Triennial Survey data, chart by the author

### Chart 2: Foreign Exchange Reserves (Figure 2)

- **type:** Stacked area chart
- **image_file:** page_02_img_01.png
- **title:** Figure 2. Foreign exchange reserves
- **page:** 2
- **data:** Composition of global central bank foreign exchange reserves by currency
- **assets/instruments:** USD reserves, EUR reserves, JPY reserves, GBP reserves, CNY reserves, Other currency reserves
- **transforms/calculations:** Percentage of total allocated reserves
- **metrics:**
  - USD share: ~70% in 2000, declined to ~60% by 2010-2015, stable since at ~58-60%
  - EUR share: ~20-25%
  - CNY/JPY/GBP/Other: small and growing modestly
- **time_range:** 2000 to ~2022
- **x_axis:** Years (2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020)
- **y_axis:** Percent (0-100%)
- **insight:** There was a meaningful reduction in USD reserves from ~70% to ~60% centered on 2003-2007 when the dollar was falling. However, since 2010 the USD share has been stable. The decline was mostly a valuation effect (weaker dollar = lower USD share) and some diversification during a period of dollar weakness. The dollar's reserve status is not under threat.
- **source:** Not explicitly stated (likely IMF COFER data)
