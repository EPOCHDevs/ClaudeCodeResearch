## Summary
This is a "Rabbit Hole" edition focused entirely on a non-market, socioeconomic topic: the impact of smartphones and social media on adolescent neurodevelopment. The piece includes a foreword by Ben Hunt (Epsilon Theory) and an introduction by Donnelly, followed by the full 13D Research & Strategy report. The research covers: smartphone usage linked to depression/suicide/substance abuse (PLOS One study of 50,000+ Korean teens), the Global Mind Project (28,000 individuals, 41 countries) showing earlier smartphone ownership correlates with worse mental health, neuroplasticity and prefrontal cortex development, dopamine/reward prediction error mechanics of social media, media multitasking effects, and neuroimaging showing brain structure changes. ### KEPT #2: Smartphone Usage vs. Mental Health Outcomes (Six-Panel)

- **type:** Six-panel line chart (dose-response curves with confidence intervals)
- **image_file:** page_06_img_00.png
- **title:** Panels labeled A through F: A. Stress perception, B. Dissatisfaction with sleep, C. Depressive symptoms, D. Suicide (suicidal idea/attempt/plan), E. Substance use (alcohol/smoking), F. Smartphone overdependence
- **page:** 6
- **data:** Odds ratios for various negative health outcomes plotted against daily smartphone usage time categories among Korean adolescents (50,000+ teens, Hanyang University Medical Center)
- **assets/instruments:** N/A (public health data)
- **transforms/calculations:** Odds ratios with 95% confidence interval bands (shaded regions)
- **metrics:**
  - Panel A (Stress): Odds ratio ~0.8-1.2 across usage categories, relatively flat
  - Panel B (Sleep dissatisfaction): Odds ratio rises sharply above 4-6 hours, reaching ~1.5-2.0 at >8 hours
  - Panel C (Depressive symptoms): Mild U-shape, odds ratio ~0.8-1.2
  - Panel D (Suicide): Three lines (suicidal idea in red, suicidal attempt in green, suicidal plan in blue). Sharp increase at >8 hours for suicidal idea and plan (~1.5)
  - Panel E (Substance use): Alcohol (yellow) and smoking (purple) odds ratios increase with usage, reaching ~2.0 at >8 hours
  - Panel F (Smartphone overdependence): Dramatic exponential increase, reaching odds ratio ~5.0-6.0 at >8 hours
- **time_range:** Cross-sectional study (published Dec 6, 2023 in PLOS One)
- **x_axis:** Smartphone usage time (hours): 0, 0-2, 2-4, 4-6, 6-8, >8
- **y_axis:** Odds ratio (various scales, typically 0-2.0, Panel F goes to 6.0)
- **insight:** There is a clear dose-response relationship between smartphone usage and multiple negative health outcomes. The effect is most dramatic for smartphone overdependence (Panel F, exponential), sleep dissatisfaction (Panel B), and substance use (Panel E). Suicidal ideation also increases with heavy use. Teens who use smartphones >8 hours/day are 66% more likely to report substance use and 22% more likely to contemplate suicide.
- **source:** PLOS One (Jong Ho Cha, Young-Jin Choi, Soorack Ryu, and Jin-Hwa Moon)

### KEPT #3: Mental Wellbeing vs. Age of First Smartphone (Global Mind Project)

- **type:** Dual-panel line chart with MHQ score legend
- **image_file:** page_07_img_00.png
- **title:** "Mental wellbeing in young adulthood (ages 18-24) improves with older age of first smartphone"
- **page:** 7
- **data:**
  - Left panel: Average Mental Health Quotient (MHQ) by age of first smartphone ownership, split by gender (Males 18-24 in blue, Females 18-24 in lighter blue)
  - Right panel: Percentage "Distressed/Struggling" by age of first smartphone ownership, split by gender
  - Bottom: MHQ Score scale legend (Distressed: -100, Struggling: -50, Enduring: 0, Managing: 50, Succeeding: 100, Thriving: 150-200)
- **assets/instruments:** N/A (mental health survey data)
- **transforms/calculations:** MHQ score (composite mental health metric), percentage distressed/struggling
- **metrics:**
  - Left panel: Males MHQ ranges from ~-10 (age 5 first smartphone) to ~40+ (age 18). Females MHQ ranges from ~-15 (age 5) to ~25 (age 18). Gender gap widens with earlier smartphone adoption.
  - Right panel: Females distressed/struggling ranges from ~70% (age 5) to ~25% (age 18). Males from ~55% (age 5) to ~20% (age 18).
- **time_range:** Cross-sectional study (Global Mind Project, ~28,000 individuals ages 18-24, 41 countries)
- **x_axis:** Age of first smartphone ownership (5-18)
- **y_axis:** Left: Average MHQ score (-20 to 50). Right: % Distressed/Struggling (0 to 80%)
- **insight:** The later a child receives their first smartphone, the better their mental health outcomes in young adulthood. The effect is particularly pronounced for females, who show worse MHQ scores and higher distress rates at every age of first ownership. Getting a smartphone at age 5 vs. age 18 represents a swing from "Distressed" to "Managing" on the MHQ scale.
- **source:** Sapien Labs (Global Mind Project / The Global Mind Report)

### KEPT #4: Brain Neuroimaging - Reward vs. Control Systems

- **type:** Scientific neuroimaging (brain scan visualization)
- **image_file:** page_10_img_00.jpeg
- **title:** (Referenced as from "The Developing Brain in the Digital Era," 2021 study)
- **page:** 10
- **data:** Brain region activation map showing reward systems (red circles) vs. control systems (blue circles) in a teen media multitasker's brain. Labeled regions include: SPC (Superior Parietal Cortex), IPL (Inferior Parietal Lobule), Amy (Amygdala), dACC (dorsal Anterior Cingulate Cortex), VTA (Ventral Tegmental Area), NAcc (Nucleus Accumbens), vmPFC (ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex), IFG (Inferior Frontal Gyrus), OFC (Orbitofrontal Cortex), Insula, Put/GP (Putamen/Globus Pallidus)
- **assets/instruments:** N/A (neuroscience data)
- **transforms/calculations:** Brain region activation mapping (fMRI or similar neuroimaging)
- **metrics:** Red circles = reward system regions (disproportionately activated); Blue circles = control system regions (relatively underactivated). A healthy brain would show approximate balance.
- **time_range:** N/A (neuroscience study, published 2021)
- **x_axis:** N/A (anatomical brain cross-sections: sagittal and coronal views)
- **y_axis:** N/A
- **insight:** In teen media multitaskers, reward systems (red) show higher activation compared to control mechanisms (blue), indicating an imbalance. A healthy brain would show approximate balance between reward and control. This provides physical evidence that excessive smartphone/media use structurally alters adolescent brain function, favoring reward-seeking behavior over executive control.
- **source:** Frontiers Media (2021 study "The Developing Brain in the Digital Era")
