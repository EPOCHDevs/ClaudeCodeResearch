- **type:** Multi-line time series chart
- **image_file:** page_02_img_00.png
- **title:** Real Policy Rates using Core CPI
- **page:** 2
- **data:** Real policy rates (nominal policy rate minus core CPI inflation) for 9 major central banks over approximately 6 years
- **assets/instruments:** Fed, ECB, BoC, BoE, SNB, RBNZ, RBA, Riksbank, Norges, Bank of Japan
- **transforms/calculations:** Real policy rate = nominal policy rate - core CPI inflation rate
- **metrics:** Range: approximately -7.0% to +3.0%; Fed at 1.7%, Riksbank at 1.9%, Bank of Japan at -3.0%
- **time_range:** Approximately 2019 to 2024/2025 (about 6 years)
- **x_axis:** Date (2019 through 2024)
- **y_axis:** Real policy rate (percentage, -7.0% to +3.0%)
- **insight:** Shows the dramatic divergence and reconvergence of real policy rates across developed market central banks. The y-axis is scaled wide to capture the COVID-era megaloose policy (-6% to -7% real rates for some banks). Currently, the Fed and Riksbank stand out as having the tightest real policy (1.7% and 1.9% respectively), while the Bank of Japan remains deeply negative at -3.0%. This sets up the context for the upcoming flurry of central bank meetings, particularly the FOMC which may sound dovish and set the table for a July cut.
