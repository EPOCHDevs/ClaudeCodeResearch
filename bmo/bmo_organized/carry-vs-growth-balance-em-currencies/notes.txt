Title: Finding the right balance between carry and growth
Source: BMO FX macro report 3-9-26.pdf (page 4)
Date: 2026-03-09
Type: chart

Insight: Scatter plot showing the relationship between carry-to-volatility ratio (x-axis) and growth factor score (y-axis) across major currencies, identifying which EM currencies offer the best risk-adjusted returns from combining carry trades with growth dynamics.

Context (above):
What’s next for carry? EM is increasingly being split by a mix of terms of trade and positioning. The latter reflects  
crowded exposure in popular carry trades such as BRL, HUF, ZAR, and IDR. Over the past six months, our global macro  
framework (MAPS) shows that the growth factor dominated market dynamics, supported by a softer USD, easier  
monetary policy, and a contained term premium—an ideal backdrop for carry. Risk-adjusted returns across several EM  
currencies were strong, reinforcing capital inflows into EM carry strategies. Is carry done? For now, we think so. While  
our positioning indicator (GPS) shows a sharp unwind in some of the most crowded EM carry trades, we do not see a  
swift resolution to the recent rise in geopolitical uncertainty. Crude oil has pushed toward $100/bbl, and Iran’s response  
to recent developments has heightened regional instability. The risk is a deepening negative feedback loop—particularly  
if tensions extend to ports, shipping lanes, or critical infrastructure—which would likely mark the next phase of the  
EMFX adjustment. That setup increasingly resembles 2022, where FX performance was driven by a terms of trade  
shock, favoring oil exporters over importers. As the second chart shows, most currencies have adjusted rationally,  
moving broadly in line with their implied terms of trade beta. However, several currencies still trade at a material  
premium, including HUF, KRW, MXN, BRL, NOK, and AUD. We think this risk premium will eventually create better  
buying opportunities, particularly in BRL and MXN, which still offer an attractive mix of growth and carry—especially  
versus LatAm peers. For now, however, we continue to prefer staying long the USD.
