Title: Global Macro Vol Trailing GeoMacro Uncertainty (2015-2025)
Source: BMO FX macro report 3-9-26.pdf (page 1)
Date: 2026-03-09
Type: chart

Insight: Global macro volatility is currently lagging behind rising geopolitical uncertainty, suggesting potential for further volatility expansion as markets reprice macro assets amid energy supply shocks and structural USD strength.

Context (above):
Exceptionalism stages a comeback. The USD has regained its safe haven appeal, signaling a return of the USD 
smile, and we think the rally has room to extend in the weeks ahead. Several forces are at work, led by rising 
geopolitical stress and uncertainty. While the USD has traded weaker versus traditional geopolitical uncertainty 
measures in recent years, that relationship is now shifting—reflecting the nature of the shock. This episode is 
increasingly about energy prices and negative supply shocks. As in 2022, the US and the USD are relatively 
insulated given energy self-reliance and favorable terms of trade position. Europe and parts of Asia sit on the other 
side of this shock, particularly with European gas inventories already low after a cold winter. Crude oil is up 
>75% from the December 2025 lows, and markets are now working through the second order effects of a rapid 
spike in energy costs. Investors remain skeptical that policy actions alone can quickly ease these constraints—most 
notably around potential disruptions to global energy supply routes. The result has been a broader repricing of global 
macro assets: higher volatility, higher yields and breakevens, a stronger USD, and weaker equities. In that 
environment, the return of USD exceptionalism looks less cyclical and more structural, especially given the stale 
narrative around USD weakness and consensus bearishness.
